# 
[Voyant Tools](https://voyant-tools.org) is a web-based tool for reading and analysing texts. You paste in a text or upload files, and it shows word frequencies, word clouds, how words are used in context, and how terms change through a text. It was created by the digital humanities scholars Stéfan Sinclair and Geoffrey Rockwell.

## How it helps in English studies

In literary analysis, close reading can be combined with distant reading. A concrete task: I upload two novels from a course, for example *Pride and Prejudice* and *Jane Eyre*, and compare their most frequent words and the contexts of a word like "duty". This gives me patterns to investigate and quote, instead of relying only on my impression.

## The five checks

- **Licence:** The code is under GPL3 and the web application content is under Creative Commons Attribution. This lets me use, study, change and share it, as long as shared changes keep the same licence.
- **Last change:** When I checked on 12 November 2026, the latest commit was 24 hours old.
- **People:** The repository has 10 contributors.
- **Questions:** 85 issues are closed and only 25 are open, so most reported problems are handled. Response times vary: some issues were answered within a week, others took months.
- **Money:** I found no information about funding. It began as an academic project, and the original repository was archived after Stéfan Sinclair passed away, with development continuing in voyanttools/Voyant.

## Verdict

I would use Voyant for exploring texts in a thesis, but not as my only method. It is actively maintained, but a small team stands behind it and its funding is unclear. I could leave it again, because I can export my results and texts, and it can be run on my own computer.
